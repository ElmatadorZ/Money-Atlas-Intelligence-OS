📍 MARKET STRUCTURE INSIGHT
The mistake is in the premise. A stop loss is measured against the size of the position, and leverage multiplies the position.

- 3% price move × 20x leverage = **60% of your margin**
- Example: $1,000 margin [ASSUMED, for illustration] at 20x is a $20,000 position. A 3% stop costs about **$600**. If that $1,000 is your whole account, one stop-out wipes out 60% of it.
- Your liquidation point is roughly a 5% move (100% ÷ 20). It's actually a bit closer once maintenance margin and fees are counted. So your 3% stop sits only a couple of percent from liquidation.

What I can't know from here: your account size, how much of it is margin, whether you use isolated or cross margin, and your exchange's maintenance margin rules. Those inputs set your real risk.

📍 KEY RISK
- **The stop works:** you lose about 60% of margin, plus fees and funding.
- **The stop slips:** in a fast move or a wick, a stop-market order can fill worse than 3%, and in a sharp gap you can be liquidated before it fills. On cross margin, the loss can reach beyond this one position.
- **Noise:** a 3% swing is ordinary for ETH. You may get stopped out often even if your idea is right.

📍 STRATEGIC TAKEAWAY
Pick your account risk first, then work out the position size from it. Leverage comes last.
Position size = (account × risk %) ÷ stop distance.
To risk 1% of a $1,000 account with a 3% stop: $10 ÷ 0.03 ≈ **$333 position**. That's about 0.33x on the whole account, not 20x. Position size is what controls your risk. Leverage only changes how much margin the exchange holds.
