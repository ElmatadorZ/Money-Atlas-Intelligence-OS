📍 **FIRST PRINCIPLE BREAKDOWN**
- **Problem:** The 3% is how far *price* moves against you. It is not how much of your *account* you lose.
- **Mechanism:** Leverage multiplies the price move. A 3% move × 20x = **60% loss on the margin** in the trade.
- **Example:** $1,000 margin × 20x = $20,000 position. A 3% move against you = **−$600**. If that $1,000 was your whole account, one stop-out takes 60% of it.
- **The formula that actually matters:**
  `Account risk % = (position size × stop distance %) ÷ account size`
  Leverage isn't in the formula. Risk is set by **position size**. Leverage only sets how little margin you need to open that size.

📍 **SYSTEM MAP: how the loss compounds**
- **Liquidation is close.** At 20x, roughly a 5% adverse move (a bit less once maintenance margin is counted, and it varies by exchange) wipes the margin. Your 3% stop sits inside that, so in normal conditions the stop fires first. In a fast move, a gap, or thin liquidity, slippage can push your fill well past 3%.
- **Noise vs. stop.** ETH often moves more than 3% inside a single day. A 3% stop on a high-volatility asset can get hit by ordinary noise, not because your idea was wrong.
- **Losing streaks hurt fast.** At −60% per loss: $1,000 → $400 → $160 after two losses in a row. Two losses in a row happen all the time, even with a good strategy.
- **Recovery gets harder.** After a 60% drawdown you need +150% just to get back to even.
- **Costs.** Fees and funding are charged on the full $20,000 position, not on your $1,000.

`Position size = (account × risk %) ÷ stop distance %`
