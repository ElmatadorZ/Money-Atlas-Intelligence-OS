**Estimated price change: about −11.74%**

Δy = +150 bp = 0.015

The formula is:

%ΔP ≈ −D_mod × Δy + ½ × C × (Δy)²

**1. Duration effect**
−8.5 × 0.015 = −0.1275, which is **−12.75%**

**2. Convexity adjustment**
½ × 90 × (0.015)² = 0.5 × 90 × 0.000225 = 0.010125, which is **+1.01%**

**3. Total**
−12.75% + 1.01% = **−11.74%**

On a bond priced at $1,000, that is a drop of about $117.40, to roughly $882.60.

**What this means:** Duration alone would put the loss at 12.75%. Convexity cuts that loss by about 1 percentage point. Positive convexity helps the bondholder when yields move by a large amount.

*Note:* This uses the standard ½ × C × (Δy)² form. Some textbooks scale convexity so the ½ is already included. If your source does that, the adjustment is 90 × 0.000225 = +2.03%, and the estimate becomes about **−10.73%**.
