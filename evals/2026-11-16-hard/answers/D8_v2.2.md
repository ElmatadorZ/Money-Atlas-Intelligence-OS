**The price falls by about 11.7%.**

Use the duration-plus-convexity approximation, with the yield change as a decimal (150 bp = 0.015):

%ΔP ≈ −(Modified duration × Δy) + ½ × Convexity × (Δy)²

- Duration effect: −8.5 × 0.015 = **−12.75%**
- Convexity adjustment: ½ × 90 × (0.015)² = 0.5 × 90 × 0.000225 = **+1.01%**
- Total: −12.75% + 1.01% ≈ **−11.74%**

For example, a bond priced at 1,000 would drop to about 882.60.

Why convexity matters here: duration alone assumes the price–yield relationship is a straight line, so it overstates the loss when yields rise (and understates the gain when they fall). For a move this large, the curve is worth about 1 percentage point, which is why the estimate goes from −12.75% to −11.74%. The result is still an approximation; repricing the bond at the new yield gives the exact figure.

One thing to check: this uses the standard convention, where convexity is quoted so that the ½ factor applies. Some sources quote convexity with the ½ already built in, or scaled differently (for example 0.90 instead of 90). In those cases the adjustment changes, so confirm which convention your source uses.
