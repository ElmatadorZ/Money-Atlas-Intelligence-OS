📍 **FIRST PRINCIPLE BREAKDOWN**
P/E = Price ÷ Earnings. A P/E can fall for two reasons:
1. **Price falls while earnings hold.** That's real cheapness.
2. **The market expects earnings to fall soon.** A trailing P/E looks low because the E is old. That's a value trap.

Some arithmetic using only your numbers:
- If earnings stayed flat over the 3 months, the P/E was about 13 ÷ 0.85 ≈ **15.3** before the drop. So the index wasn't expensive before it fell. It was already a little below its average.
- If the P/E goes back to 16 and earnings hold, that's **+23%** from here (16 ÷ 13 − 1).
- If earnings fall 20%, today's price is a P/E of 13 ÷ 0.8 ≈ **16.25**. That's slightly above average, so it's no longer cheap.
- The break-even point is earnings falling about **19%** (1 − 13/16). Below that, the "cheap" case disappears.

**The real question isn't "is 13 below 16?" It's "will earnings fall more or less than 19%?"**
