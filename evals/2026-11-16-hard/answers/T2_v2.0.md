📍 **FIRST PRINCIPLE BREAKDOWN — the math behind "2 × 8% = 16%"**

Leverage doesn't double your return. It doubles your **excess return over the borrowing cost**, and it multiplies your **volatility drag** by about 4.

Assume your 8% is a compound annual rate (CAGR) and use the standard approximation, CAGR ≈ average return − σ²/2:

| Step | Unlevered | 2x levered |
|---|---|---|
| Volatility | 10% | **20%** |
| Average (arithmetic) return | 8% + 0.5% = 8.5% | 2 × 8.5% − *b* = 17% − *b* |
| Volatility drag (σ²/2) | 0.5% | **2.0%** |
| **Expected compound return** | **8%** | **≈ 15% − *b*** |

*b* is your annual borrowing rate on the borrowed half of the position.

| Borrowing cost *b* | Expected 2x CAGR (before fees and taxes) | Hits 15%? |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | ~15% | Only if borrowing were free |
| 3% | ~12% | No |
| 5% | ~10% | No |
| 6% | ~9% | No, and you've barely beaten 8% while doubling the risk |

**Breakeven rule:** 2x leverage only adds return if **b < (unlevered CAGR − 1%)**. With 8% CAGR, you need to borrow below about 7%. To actually reach **15%**, you need borrowing at about **0%**, *and* the past 8% has to repeat. Neither one is a safe assumption.

Forward sensitivity: if 60/40 returns 6% from here instead of 8%, 2x levered gives about 11% − *b*. At *b* = 5–6% that's 5–6%, which is **worse than not levering, with twice the risk.**

📍 **DECISION FRAMEWORK**
- **IF** you can't borrow below about 3% → 2x won't get you near 15%, and the extra return probably isn't worth twice the risk.
- **IF** you couldn't stay invested through a −50% drop without selling or getting a margin call → don't run 2x. Look at 1.2–1.5x, which gives about 9–10% expected with much smaller drawdowns, or just raise your equity share.
- **IF** you go ahead anyway → use financing with no margin-call trigger or a wide buffer (for example futures with plenty of spare cash, or a fixed-rate loan), rebalance back to your target leverage, and set a firm maximum-drawdown rule before you start.
- **IF** 15% is a must-have → that's a target problem, not a leverage problem. No portfolio built on an 8%/10% base can promise 15% without taking on a real chance of a large permanent loss. Consider changing the target, your savings rate, or your time horizon.
