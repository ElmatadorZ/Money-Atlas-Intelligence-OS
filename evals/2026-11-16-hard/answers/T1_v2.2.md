**Short answer: cheaper than its own history, yes. Cheap in the sense of "likely to go back up", not proven yet.** A P/E of 13 against a 16 average means the market is priced about 19% below its 10-year norm. Whether that is a bargain depends on the "E", not the "P".

📍 **MARKET STRUCTURE INSIGHT**
- **Premise check:** a 15% fall plus a low P/E is not, on its own, a signal. P/E = price / earnings. If analysts are still cutting earnings forecasts, today's 13 on *trailing* earnings can turn into 15–16 on *next year's* earnings without the index moving at all. That is the classic value trap.
- **Is the 16 average still the right anchor?** A 10-year average only works as a target if the market's growth, sector mix and fund flows are the same as they were. If the SET has gone through a lasting de-rating (slower growth, a heavy weight in old-economy sectors, steady foreign selling), the "normal" P/E may now sit below 16.
- **Known:** your figures (−15% in 3 months, P/E 13 vs 16). **Not known from here:** whether 13 is trailing or forward, the direction of earnings revisions, the current Thai bond yield, and foreign fund flows. I have no live data and I'm not adding any figures of my own.

📍 **KEY RISK**
Earnings are falling faster than price. **Invalidation of the "cheap" view:** forward EPS forecasts keep getting cut, so the forward P/E climbs back toward or above 16.

📍 **STRATEGIC TAKEAWAY**
- **Re-rating path:** earnings revisions level off or turn up, and foreign selling eases. Arithmetic only: if earnings hold flat and P/E returns to 16, the index rises about 23% (16 ÷ 13). That is a calculation, not a forecast.
- **Value-trap path:** EPS cuts continue, and 13 turns out to be fair value for a lower-growth market.
- **What to check first:** the forward P/E and the 3-month trend in EPS revisions, then the earnings yield (1/13 ≈ 7.7%) against the Thai 10-year bond yield.

If you're adding money, buying in stages (DCA) fits this kind of uncertainty better than trying to catch the bottom in one go. How much to put in depends on your time horizon and how big a further drop you can handle.

**CONFIDENCE: LOW–MEDIUM.** Valuation below the long-run average gives some edge over a multi-year horizon. The direction of earnings, which decides the outcome, is not in the data you gave.
