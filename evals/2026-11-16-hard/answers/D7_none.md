**Fair 1-year forward: about 35.80 THB per USD**

Covered interest parity gives the forward:

F = S × (1 + r_THB) / (1 + r_USD)
F = 36.50 × 1.02 / 1.04 = 37.23 / 1.04 ≈ **35.80**

- Forward points: 35.80 − 36.50 ≈ **−0.70** (about −700 pips)
- With continuous compounding you get 36.50 × e^(−0.02) ≈ 35.78. The difference comes from the compounding convention, and the idea is the same.

**Does this mean the baht will weaken?**

No, on both counts.

1. **The forward points toward a stronger baht, not a weaker one.** 35.80 is below spot, so it takes fewer baht to buy a dollar a year forward. The baht trades at a forward premium and the dollar at a forward discount.

2. **The forward is not a forecast in any case.** It is a no-arbitrage price, not what the market expects. It has to sit at 35.80 because otherwise you could make a riskless profit. You could borrow THB at 2%, convert to USD, invest at 4% and sell the USD forward. The forward has to take back exactly that 2% rate gap. So the forward only tells you about the interest rate difference, not where USDTHB will actually trade.

**What the evidence says:** The forward has historically been a poor predictor of future spot rates. High-yield currencies often fail to weaken as much as the forward implies, and sometimes they strengthen (the "forward premium puzzle"). That is why carry trades can make money on average while still carrying the risk of a sharp unwind. In practice, the actual USDTHB rate a year from now will depend on things like BoT and Fed policy, Thailand's current account (tourism, exports), capital flows, the broad dollar trend and gold prices. The forward does not capture any of these.

**Practical takeaway**

- **Thai importers who owe USD** can lock in about 35.80 today, which is cheaper than spot. That benefit is simply the rate gap being priced in, not a free gain.
- **Exporters who receive USD** who hedge forward get about 0.70 THB less per dollar than spot. That is the cost of hedging, not a forecast of loss.
- Whether to hedge should depend on how much currency risk you can tolerate, not on reading the forward as a market prediction.
