📍 **SITUATION MAP**

The hypothetical: the Strait of Hormuz is closed to tanker traffic for about 14 days, then reopens.

What the answer depends on is not "closed or open" but four things:
1. **Physical or insurance closure?** Mines or blockade stop the flow. War-risk premiums and shipowners refusing to sail only slow it.
2. **How much bypass capacity actually works?** Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline to the Red Sea and the UAE's Habshan–Fujairah line can reroute part of the flow, but not most of it `[UNVERIFIED: exact spare capacity]`.
3. **Do governments release reserves?** IEA coordinated stock releases and the US SPR.
4. **Is the two-week end date known in advance?** Markets price the *expected* duration, not the actual one. On day 1 nobody knows it will be 14 days.

Scale reference: about one fifth of global oil consumption and a large share of LNG trade (Qatar) pass through Hormuz `[UNVERIFIED: ~20% oil, ~20% LNG]`. No other single chokepoint carries that much.

---

📍 **FIRST PRINCIPLE BREAKDOWN**

- **Problem:** Supply is removed instantly, and demand can't adjust in two weeks. Oil demand is very inelastic in the short run.
- **Cause:** When supply is inelastic in the short run, the price has to jump far enough to destroy marginal demand or pull oil out of storage.
- **Mechanism:** Supply doesn't vanish. It goes *in transit* or gets stuck. Two weeks of lost flow can be covered by inventories and strategic reserves. **The price spike is mostly a risk premium plus panic over logistics, not a true shortage.** That is why a two-week event behaves differently from a two-month event.
- **Leverage:** The asymmetry sits in the **reopening**, not the closure. The spike is crowded and obvious. The unwind is what catches people.
- **Outcome:** Expect a sharp spike, then a fade. The fade starts once the end becomes credible, not when ships actually move again.

📍 **SMC LAYER MAP**

The layers apply to *market behaviour around the event*, not to a specific chart (I have no live structure):

- **Oil:** The closure headline forces an **L2 Expansion** spike. Smart Money that was already long or holding options sells into the spike. The approach to reopening is **L4 Distribution**: fake breakouts above the first spike high while volume fades. When reopening is confirmed, it becomes **L5 Exit Liquidity**, with retail buying the "oil to the moon" headline at the top.
- **Gold:** Already a slower, structural trend. The shock adds a liquidity grab (a sweep of highs or a margin-call sweep of lows) inside the existing trend. Watch for a **sweep-and-reclaim**.
- **SET:** The panic sell-off becomes a possible **L1 Accumulation** zone in the hardest-hit quality names, *if* the closure is clearly temporary.
- **USD:** A spike inside its existing regime. It doesn't change the trend unless the Fed turns hawkish.

**Invalidation for this map:** closure extends past about 4 weeks, or attacks hit Gulf production infrastructure (not just shipping). Then it's no longer a "temporary premium". It becomes a **real supply loss**, and the layers shift to a new L2 trend.

📍 **SCENARIOS**

🐂 **Bull (for risk assets) / Quick de-escalation**
- Trigger: a credible reopening timeline within the first week, an IEA release announced, a partial flow via escorts.
- Oil: the spike fully reverses within days of the reopening signal. Gold gives back part of the gain. USD fades. **SET rebounds hard**, led by airlines, tourism and petrochemicals.
- Entry logic: accumulate the hardest-hit SET quality names *after* the reopening signal is confirmed, not before.
- Invalidation: any new attack or mining event after the "reopening" headline.

🐻 **Bear / Closure holds 2 weeks + no end date until late**
- Trigger: no diplomatic path visible, insurers withdraw cover, Asian refiners cut runs.
- Oil holds a large premium for most of the period, with a steep backwardation curve. Gold firm. USD strong. THB weak. SET grinds lower, and only energy names hold up.
- Target logic: "lower highs on every bounce" in SET until the closure ends.
- Condition: inflation fear pushes central banks hawkish, which turns this into a stagflation scare.

⚖️ **Base (most probable path under this hypothetical)**
- Spike → plateau → fade. Oil peaks early, chops in week 2 as reserves and rerouting kick in, then unwinds most of the premium in the 1–3 weeks after reopening.
- Gold: net higher over the whole episode, but much less dramatic than oil.
- USD: up during the closure, back to trend after.
- SET: underperforms during the closure, then recovers. Energy outperforms during it and underperforms after.

🌪️ **Chaos / Tail risk**
- The closure spreads into strikes on Gulf oil and gas facilities, or into a wider regional war.
- No longer a 2-week event. Oil reprices to a new regime. Global stagflation risk. Gold strongest, USD strong, EM including Thailand hit hardest.
- **This tail risk is what makes the whole analysis fragile.** Size every position as if it could happen.

📍 **DECISION FRAMEWORK**

- **IF your timeframe is days (trader):** The edge is in the *fade*, not the spike. Shorting the oil spike before the end is credible is dangerous, because gap risk is extreme. Wait for the reopening signal plus a failed retest of the high (L4 → L5) before fading.
- **IF your timeframe is months (investor):** Don't chase energy at the peak of the headline. Treat a SET panic as a possible accumulation window for quality names hurt by the shock, *only if* the closure is clearly temporary.
- **IF you hold gold as a long-term hedge:** This event confirms why you hold it. It's not a reason to add aggressively at the spike.
- **IF your risk tolerance is low:** Cut position sizes and hold cash in USD or short-term instruments during the closure. Avoid leverage, because spreads widen and stops get skipped (gapped over).
- **IF you run a Thai business with energy or USD costs** (e.g. importing inputs, shipping): hedge FX and fuel costs early. Costs go up before they come down.

---

📍 **RISK & FAILURE MODE**

This entire analysis is invalid if:
1. **The closure is longer than ~4 weeks.** Inventories and reserves run thin, and the "premium fades" logic breaks.
2. **Production infrastructure is damaged**, not just shipping lanes. Then the loss is real supply, not delayed supply.
3. **Central banks react hawkishly** to the inflation impulse. Then gold's upside is capped, the USD is much stronger, and equities fall harder.
4. **The global economy is already weak.** Then demand destruction dominates, and oil can crash *below* pre-shock levels soon after reopening.
5. **Positioning is unknown.** If the market was already heavily long oil before the event, the spike is smaller and the unwind is more violent.
