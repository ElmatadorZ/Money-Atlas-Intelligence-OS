**Bottom line:** the sign of every move depends on *why* the market thinks the Fed cut 50 bp by surprise. If it reads as "the Fed is getting ahead of a slowdown", you get a weaker dollar and a broad rally in risk assets and gold. If it reads as "the Fed knows something is breaking", the dollar can rise and bitcoin and EM currencies can fall, even though rates went down. Day 1 usually shows the easing reflex. Days 2–5 show which story the market has settled on.

📍 SITUATION MAP
- **Given:** a hypothetical 50 bp cut the market did not expect, announced tomorrow.
- **Not known here:** the current fed funds rate, how much easing is already priced in, today's DXY, gold, BTC and EM levels, and whether tomorrow is a scheduled FOMC meeting. An *intermeeting* cut would signal an emergency and pushes everything toward the panic case below. I have no live data, so this map has no price levels.

📍 FIRST PRINCIPLE BREAKDOWN
What drives the reaction is the **surprise relative to what was priced**, not the 50 bp itself. A surprise cut does two things:
1. **Rate channel:** lower expected US yields shrink the dollar's carry advantage and cut the cost of holding assets that pay no yield (gold, BTC).
2. **Information channel:** the Fed is telling markets it sees more weakness than they thought. If that signal is the bigger one, investors move into safety and dollar liquidity, which works against channel 1.

📍 SYSTEM MAP
Fed surprise → front-end yields fall, curve steepens → rate gap narrows → DXY ↓ (channel 1) or ↑ (channel 2) → global dollar liquidity → gold (fastest and most direct), BTC (liquidity plus risk appetite, so higher beta), EM FX (carry plus growth, the most sensitive to which channel wins).

📍 SCENARIOS — first week

🐂 **"Insurance cut" (Fed ahead of the curve)**
*Trigger:* the statement and press conference frame it as preemptive, equities and credit hold up, and the yield curve steepens without credit spreads widening.
- DXY: falls, most against the EUR and JPY, where rate gaps narrow the most.
- Gold: rises. It is the cleanest winner from lower real yields.
- BTC: rises, usually more than gold in percentage terms, on liquidity.
- EM FX: high-carry and current-account-deficit currencies gain the most. In Asia, the THB, KRW and IDR tend to firm.

🐻 **"Panic cut" (something is breaking)**
*Trigger:* an intermeeting move, weak data or a credit or bank event behind it, equities falling, credit spreads widening.
- DXY: dips at first, then may *rise* as investors scramble for dollar funding. The JPY and CHF can outperform it.
- Gold: rises, perhaps after a brief dip on liquidation selling to raise cash.
- BTC: often falls with equities in a risk-off flush, then has to show whether it trades like liquidity or like risk.
- EM FX: weakens despite the narrower rate gap as money leaves EM, with the high-beta and deficit currencies hit hardest.

⚖️ **Base: day-1 easing reflex, then a partial fade**
This is most likely when the cut is scheduled and the guidance is calm. The initial dollar drop and the rallies in gold, BTC and EM partly reverse by mid-week as markets ask what the Fed saw. Gold keeps more of its gain than BTC or EM FX.

📍 WHAT TO WATCH (it tells you which scenario you are in)
1. **Credit spreads and equities on days 1–2.** If spreads are tight and stocks are up, it is an insurance cut. If both are falling, it is a panic cut.
2. **The 2s10s curve.** A steepening driven by the 2-year falling is benign. A steepening driven by the 10-year rising suggests worries about inflation or Fed credibility, which is good for gold and mixed for the dollar.
3. **USD/JPY.** A sharp drop in USD/JPY alongside falling stocks points to carry trades unwinding, which is bad for EM FX.
4. **Fed guidance.** "Further cuts likely" deepens the dollar's fall. "One and done" brings a fade by day 3–5.

📍 RISK & FAILURE MODE
- If the cut is already partly priced in, the "surprise" is small and every effect shrinks or reverses on day 1 ("buy the rumour, sell the news").
- An ECB, BoJ or PBoC move the same week can override the DXY reaction, because DXY is mostly the EUR.
- **Invalidation:** this map stops working if DXY rises *and* gold falls together in the first 2–3 days. That combination points to a dollar-funding squeeze, not easing, and calls for a liquidity-crisis framework.
