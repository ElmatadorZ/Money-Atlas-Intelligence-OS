**Short answer:** The SET is cheaper than its own history, but that alone doesn't make it cheap. A P/E of 13 against an average of 16 is a discount of about 19% (13 ÷ 16 ≈ 0.81). Whether that's a bargain or a value trap depends on the "E", meaning earnings, and on whether the 10-year average is still the right yardstick.

📍 **MARKET STRUCTURE INSIGHT**
- **The fall alone explains the discount.** If earnings held flat, a 15% drop takes the P/E from about 15.3 to 13 (13 ÷ 0.85 ≈ 15.3). So three months ago the market was trading close to its average. It wasn't expensive, and now it's simply less expensive.
- **Which P/E is it?** If 13 is trailing and analysts are cutting forward earnings, the real multiple is higher than it looks. A 15% earnings cut at today's price puts the P/E back near 15.3, which is roughly the average again and not cheap at all.
- **Is 16 still the right anchor?** A market can trade below its old average for years if its growth outlook, sector mix or foreign investor appetite has changed. Mean reversion is a tendency, not a law.
- **What I don't know:** your figures are taken as given, and I have no live data. That means I can't see current forward-earnings revisions, today's Thai bond yields (needed to judge the earnings yield of 1/13 ≈ 7.7% against bonds) or fund flows.

📍 **KEY RISK**
The main risk is a value trap, where a low P/E sits on top of falling earnings. **Invalidation:** consensus forward earnings keep getting cut. Then the "discount" is just the market pricing in lower profits ahead of time.

📍 **STRATEGIC TAKEAWAY**
- **Re-rating path:** earnings estimates stabilise or rise and foreign flows turn positive. If the P/E returns to 16 on flat earnings, that's about +23% (16 ÷ 13 ≈ 1.23).
- **Value-trap path:** earnings downgrades continue and the P/E stays around 13 or lower on a shrinking E. The index can then keep falling even though it "looks cheap".

The deciding check is the direction of forward earnings revisions, not the P/E number. If you invest, go in gradually (for example in staged buys) rather than all at once, and size the position for the value-trap case. The decision is yours; for personal advice, see a licensed adviser.
