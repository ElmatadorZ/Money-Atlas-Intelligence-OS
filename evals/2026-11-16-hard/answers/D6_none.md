Probably not, at least not on that record alone. "Called 9 of the last 10 tops" sounds great, but it leaves out the information you need to decide.

**1. How many times did it fire in total?**
This matters most. If it fired 60 times and 9 of those were near real tops, then most of its signals were false alarms, and each one would have pushed you out of a rising market. Plenty of indicators "predicted" every crash because they fire all the time. Ask: *when it fires, how often is a top actually close?* That is a very different number from "how many tops did it catch."

**2. How close counts as "calling" a top?**
If an indicator fires 6 to 18 months early, it can be technically right and still cost you money. Markets often rise another 20 to 30% after a warning. Selling early and missing that run can leave you worse off than riding out the drop.

**3. Was it fitted to the past?**
Many indicators are designed or tuned *after the fact* to match known tops. Then it is no surprise they look great on those tops. The real test is how it did on data it was not built on, ideally after it was published.

**4. Ten tops is a small sample.**
Nine out of ten is not enough evidence to stake your whole portfolio on, especially since market conditions change over time.

**5. Selling everything is really two bets.**
You have to get out at the right time *and* get back in at the right time. Does the indicator tell you when to buy back? Many people who sell before a drop never get back in and miss the recovery. Selling can also trigger taxes and trading costs.

**A more reasonable approach if you trust it somewhat:**
- Test it yourself: count every signal, measure the return 3, 6, and 12 months after each one, and compare that to simply holding.
- Use it to **adjust risk, not to go all-in or all-out**. For example, trim equity exposure by 10 to 25%, rebalance, or hedge when it fires.
- Write down your re-entry rules before you act.
- Make sure your portfolio is one you could hold through a 30 to 50% drop, so no single signal has to be right.
