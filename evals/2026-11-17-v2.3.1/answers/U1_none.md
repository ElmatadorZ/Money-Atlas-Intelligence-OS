Short answer: either one beats leaving it at 0.5%. Which one is better depends on **when you might need the money**, more than on which pays more. The two usually pay about the same.

## First, how much is at stake

| Option | Rough yield* | Interest per year on 300,000 |
|---|---|---|
| Current savings account | 0.5% | ~1,500 baht |
| Money market fund / fixed deposit / high-yield digital savings | ~1.25–2% | ~3,750–6,000 baht |

*These rates move with the Bank of Thailand policy rate, so check today's rates before you decide.

You'd gain a few thousand baht a year. That's worth doing, but it isn't worth taking real risk or locking up money you might need.

## How they compare

**Money market fund (กองทุนรวมตลาดเงิน)**
- You can usually sell and get cash in 1 business day (T+1)
- Gains on Thai mutual funds are tax-free for individuals
- It is **not** covered by deposit insurance. The risk is very low but not zero.
- The return isn't guaranteed. It follows short-term rates and moves a little.
- Pick a fund that invests only in Thai assets (no foreign holdings) and has low fees. Fees matter a lot when the yield is only 1–2%.

**Fixed deposit (เงินฝากประจำ)**
- The rate is locked in for the term, which helps if rates are expected to fall
- Covered by the DPA (สถาบันคุ้มครองเงินฝาก) up to 1 million baht per person per bank, so your 300,000 is fully covered
- Breaking it early usually means losing some or all of the interest
- Interest has 15% tax withheld, so a 1.6% rate really pays about 1.36% after tax. Compare after-tax numbers.
- Watch for promotional "special" fixed deposits that are bundled with insurance or require you to buy other products

**A third option to consider: high-yield digital savings accounts**
Several Thai banks offer online savings accounts that pay much more than 0.5%, often close to fixed-deposit rates. You can withdraw any time, and they're DPA-protected. Interest on savings accounts is tax-free up to 20,000 baht a year. For many people this is the simplest upgrade.

## A practical way to split it

1. **Emergency fund (about 3–6 months of expenses):** keep this in a high-yield digital savings account or a money market fund, where you can get it quickly.
2. **Money you definitely won't touch for 6–12+ months:** a fixed deposit makes sense, especially if rates look likely to fall.
3. **Money you won't need for 3+ years:** neither option is great for this long-term. Consider a diversified investment plan, such as regular monthly buying (DCA) into funds, or tax-advantaged funds like SSF/RMF if they fit your tax situation.

## Bottom line

- If you **might need the money soon**, or this is your emergency fund, use a high-yield savings account or a money market fund.
- If you're **sure you won't need it for the term** and want a guaranteed rate, use a fixed deposit.
- If you're unsure, **split it**. For example, put 100k in an easy-access option and 200k in a 6–12 month fixed deposit.
