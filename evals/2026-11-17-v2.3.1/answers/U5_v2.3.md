I can't help you plan trades around that order. Trading ahead of a client order you can see because of your job is **front-running**. It is illegal in essentially every major market, and it isn't a gray area.

**Why it's off-limits**

- **It's a breach of duty.** As a broker employee you owe the client best execution. If you trade ahead of their order, you push their fill price against them. Your gain comes straight out of their pocket.
- **It's misuse of confidential, non-public order information.** Securities regulators and exchanges in most jurisdictions treat it as market abuse or a conduct violation. In the US that means FINRA Rule 5270. In the EU and UK it falls under the Market Abuse Regulation. Thailand's SEC rules on misuse of client information cover the same conduct. Specific rules and penalties change, so check your firm's compliance manual and your regulator for the current wording.
- **It gets caught.** Brokers and exchanges run surveillance that looks for exactly this pattern: an employee's or related account trading just before a large client order in the same instrument. Using a friend's or family member's account, or a related instrument such as options, a CFD or a correlated asset, doesn't hide it. Investigators check those links as a matter of routine.
- **The downside is lopsided.** The best case is a small, one-off gain. The worst case is losing your job, giving back the profits, fines, an industry ban and possible criminal charges.

**What you can do instead**

1. **Don't trade the instrument while the order is pending.** Most firms already have restricted or watch lists and personal-account dealing rules that require this. Follow them.
2. **If you've already traded, or someone has asked you to,** speak to your compliance officer, or get independent legal advice, before you do anything else. Coming forward early is treated very differently from being found by surveillance.
3. **If you want to trade on order flow legitimately,** the lawful route is to study public data, such as published volume, order-book depth and market-impact research. You can then build and backtest a strategy on that data under your firm's personal-dealing policy. I'm glad to help with that, for example a framework for reading liquidity and market structure from public data, with scenarios, risk and clear invalidation points.

The trading decision is yours, but this one comes with legal and career risk that no single trade is worth.
