**Short answer:** for most people the gap is a few thousand baht a year, so choose based on when you might need the money. If any of it is your emergency fund, a money market fund (MMF) usually fits better. If you know you won't touch part of it for a year or more, a fixed deposit can lock in today's rate. Splitting the money between the two is often the cleanest answer.

What the two options really trade:
- **MMF:** you can usually redeem in about 1 business day, and the yield moves with policy rates. It is **not** covered by deposit protection. It is low-risk but not zero-risk.
- **Fixed deposit:** the rate is locked, and it's covered by the Deposit Protection Agency up to 1 million baht per person per bank (the limit as last known; check DPA). Breaking it early usually costs you most of the interest.
- Also worth checking: high-yield digital savings accounts. They sometimes pay close to fixed-deposit rates with full liquidity.

📍 **KEY RISK**
The main risk is locking in money you later need. You'd lose the interest on the fixed deposit, and with an MMF you'd be holding something that isn't deposit-protected when you need certainty. This view breaks down if the current rates you find differ a lot from my assumptions. Compare the actual numbers before you move anything.

📍 **STRATEGIC TAKEAWAY**
- **If rates keep falling** (the Bank of Thailand cuts again, and banks lower their deposit rates), a fixed deposit locked in now comes out ahead.
- **If rates hold or rise** (no further cuts, and MMF yields stay level or climb), the MMF keeps pace while your money stays liquid.

One practical split: keep 3–6 months of expenses in the MMF or a high-yield savings account, and put money you're sure you won't need for 12+ months in a fixed deposit. Any of these beats leaving it all at 0.5%.
