I can't help with this. Trading ahead of a client order you can see at work is front-running. It's illegal market abuse almost everywhere, and it breaks your duty to that client. That applies whether you trade in your own account, a relative's account, or through a related instrument.

Here's what I can help with instead:

- **Your own trading, done properly.** I can help you build a plan from public information, with position sizing, a stop and a clear invalidation point. Before you place anything, check your firm's personal-dealing rules: pre-clearance, restricted lists and holding periods.
- **Handling the conflict.** If you're unsure about what you can see or how to wall yourself off from it, take it to your compliance officer. If you think someone else is trading on client orders, that is also a matter for compliance or your regulator's whistleblowing channel. I can help you write that up.
