| Option | Stop | Profit locked (per oz) | Trade-off |

**The best level is structural.** Put the stop just below the most recent higher low on the timeframe you trade, plus a small buffer. If that low sits between 4,245 and 4,280, it's a better stop than either ATR number, because a break below it means the uptrend has actually failed, not just wobbled.

**Another option is to take partial profit.** Close a third to half the position at 4,350, which banks real gains, then move the stop on the rest to breakeven. This gets you closest to "not giving back the gains" without being shaken out of the whole position.

**Two ways this can play out**
- **The trend continues:** price makes a new high and then a higher low. Move the stop up below each new higher low, and never move it back down.
- **It pulls back:** your trailed stop is hit and you keep whatever it locked in. A daily close back below 4,200 would mean the move from your entry has failed.

**Risks to keep in mind:** gold can gap over weekends and on major data releases such as CPI, payrolls and Fed decisions. A stop guarantees an exit, not a price, so in a gap you can be filled worse than your level.
