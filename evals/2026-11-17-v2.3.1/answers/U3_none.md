You're up $150 per ounce. The first thing to settle is that "not giving back gains" and "staying in the trade" pull against each other. The closer your stop is to price, the more profit it protects, but the more likely normal gold noise knocks you out before the move is done. So the right stop depends on what you're trying to do.

**Three ways to set it, from loosest to tightest**

1. **Breakeven-plus: around 4,210–4,220**
   - This makes the trade risk-free. Set it a little above 4,200 so spread, commission and swap are covered.
   - You give up almost all of the current gain if it hits, but you can no longer lose money.
   - This is the minimum step for most traders once a trade has moved this far.

2. **Below structure: under the most recent higher low**
   - Look at the timeframe you entered on. Find the last swing low that came before the push to 4,350, and put your stop a few dollars below it.
   - Below a break of that level, the uptrend you bought is in question anyway, so this is usually the most logical place for a stop.
   - Round numbers like 4,300 often act as support. Put stops slightly *below* them (for example 4,290–4,295), not right on them, because stops bunched at round numbers tend to get run.

3. **Volatility trail: about 1.5–2× ATR below price**
   - Check the current ATR on your chart. If daily ATR is around $50, a 2× trail from 4,350 sits near 4,250. A 1.5× trail sits near 4,275.
   - Raise it only as price makes new highs. Never lower it.
   - This scales the stop to how much gold is actually moving right now, which is better than picking a fixed dollar amount.

**A practical combination**
- Move the stop to breakeven-plus (~4,215) **now**, so this can't turn into a loss.
- If you want to keep part of the gain, take partial profit (say a third to half) here. That locks in real money without making the stop so tight it gets clipped.
- Trail the rest with either structure or ATR, whichever is lower, so it has room to run.

**What to avoid**
- **Stops just $20–30 under price** (for example 4,325). Gold regularly moves that much within a day, especially around US data, Fed speakers and the London/NY sessions. You'll probably be stopped out on noise.
- **Moving the stop down** if price pulls back. A stop only goes one way.
- **Ignoring event risk.** A stop is not guaranteed over a weekend or across a big release, because price can gap through it. If CPI, NFP or an FOMC decision is close, consider cutting size instead of relying only on the stop.
