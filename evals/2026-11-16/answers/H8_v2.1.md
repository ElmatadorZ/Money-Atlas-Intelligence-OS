**Bitcoin peaked at about $69,000 on 10 November 2021, then fell roughly 77% to about $15,500 by late November 2022.**

- **The peak:** The intraday all-time high was around $68,800–$69,000, depending on the exchange, on 10 November 2021. The highest daily close was a little lower, about $67,500 on 8 November. Earlier that year, in April 2021, it had already topped out once at about $64,800.
- **The fall:** The bear market went in stages. The Terra/LUNA collapse in May 2022 came first. Then Celsius and Three Arrows Capital failed in June, which took BTC below $20,000. The bottom came when FTX collapsed in November 2022, with an intraday low of about $15,500 around 21 November. From about $69,000 to about $15,500 is a drop of roughly 77–78%, about a year after the peak.

**Why the fall was so deep:** The 2021 rally ran on near-zero interest rates and heavy leverage across crypto lenders and exchanges. In 2022 the Fed raised rates quickly. That pulled money out of risk assets, and the borrowed money behind the rally had to be paid back fast. Each big failure forced more selling. Bitcoin's earlier peak-to-bottom drops were about 84% (2017–18) and about 86% (2013–15). By that standard, 2022 was in fact a little milder.

*These figures come from my training data. Exact highs and lows differ slightly between exchanges and data providers.*
