I can't see your chart, timeframe, or account size, so I can't give you one right number. Price alone doesn't tell you where a stop belongs. Here's how to work it out for your setup at $3,000.

**1. Put the stop where your trade idea is proven wrong**
A stop should sit at the price where your reason for going long no longer holds, not at a round number or a percentage you picked.
- **Swing low / structure:** Find the most recent higher low or demand zone that your long depends on. Place the stop just below it, with a buffer so a wick doesn't take you out. If that low is $2,880, a stop around $2,850 makes sense.
- **Liquidity sweeps:** Obvious lows and round numbers ($2,900, $2,800) attract stop hunts. Put your stop a little past the level where the sweep would run, not right on it.

**2. Check the distance against volatility (ATR)**
ETH often moves 3–5% in a day. Look at the ATR on the timeframe you're trading:
- A stop tighter than about 1× ATR will likely get hit by normal noise.
- 1.5–2× ATR below entry is a common range. With a daily ATR near $120, that puts the stop around $2,760–$2,820.
- If the structure stop and the ATR stop are far apart, go with the structure stop and reduce your position size.

**3. Size the position to the stop, not the other way around**
Decide how much of your account you'll risk, usually 0.5–2%, and calculate size from that:

> Position size = (Account × Risk %) ÷ (Entry − Stop)

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk ($100), entry $3,000, stop $2,850 (a $150 gap). That gives a position of about 0.67 ETH. A wider stop means a smaller position. Moving the stop tighter just so you can hold a bigger position is how most accounts get wiped out.

**4. Rough guide by trading style**
| Style | Typical stop distance from $3,000 |
|---|---|
| Scalp / intraday (15m–1h) | 1–2% (~$2,940–$2,970) |
| Swing (4h–daily) | 4–8% (~$2,760–$2,880) |
| Position (daily–weekly) | 10–20%, below major weekly support |

**5. Before you enter**
- Is the reward at least 2× the risk to your first target? If not, skip the trade.
- If you're using leverage, make sure your liquidation price is well below your stop.
- Use a hard stop order rather than a mental stop. Crypto trades 24/7 and can move sharply while you're asleep.
- Once price moves about 1R in your favor, you can move the stop to breakeven or trail it under new higher lows.
