Bitcoin peaked at roughly **$69,000 on 10 November 2021**. That was the intraday all-time high; exchanges differ slightly, and many data sites record about $68,800–$69,000. Earlier that year it had reached a high of about $64,000 in April.

**How far it fell:** over the next year it dropped to about **$15,500 in November 2022**, during the FTX collapse. That is a fall of roughly **77%** from the peak: (69,000 − 15,500) ÷ 69,000 ≈ 0.78.

**What drove the fall:** in 2022 the Fed raised interest rates quickly, which drained liquidity from speculative assets. On top of that came a run of crypto failures: Terra/LUNA in May, then Celsius and Three Arrows Capital, and finally FTX in November. Each one forced more selling.

**Why it matters:** a drop of 75–85% from a cycle peak has happened to Bitcoin several times (2014–15, 2018, 2022). If you hold it, plan for that size of loss. Size the position so you could sit through a drop like that without being forced to sell.

These figures are historical (last known from my training data) and vary a little by exchange. If you need exact numbers, check a price-history source such as CoinMarketCap or CoinGecko.
