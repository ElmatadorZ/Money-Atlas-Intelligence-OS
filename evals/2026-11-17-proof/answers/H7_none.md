## 1. Put the stop where your trade idea is proven wrong

A stop shouldn't be a random percentage. It should sit at the price where the reason you went long no longer holds.

- **Swing low:** Find the most recent higher low that the move is building from. Put the stop a little below it, not exactly on it. Many traders set stops at the obvious level, and price often sweeps it before reversing.
- **Support or demand zone:** If you're buying a retest of support (for example, a zone around $2,900–2,950), the stop goes below the bottom of that zone, not inside it.
- **Breakout:** If you're buying a breakout above resistance, the stop goes back below the level that broke. If price falls back under it, the breakout failed.

## 2. Check it against volatility (ATR)

ETH often moves 3–5% in a day. A stop tighter than normal noise will get hit even when your idea is right.

- A common rule is **1.5–2× ATR** below entry, on the timeframe you're trading.
- Rough ranges at $3,000:
  - **Intraday/scalp (15m–1H):** about 1–2% → $2,940–2,970
  - **Swing (4H–Daily):** about 4–8% → $2,760–2,880
  - **Position (Daily–Weekly):** about 10–15% → $2,550–2,700

These are only typical ranges. Your actual stop should come from the structure in step 1.

## 3. Size the position to fit the stop

Choose the stop first, then work out how big the position can be. Don't pick the size first and squeeze the stop to fit it.

**Position size = (Account × risk %) ÷ (Entry − Stop)**

Example: $10,000 account, 1% risk ($100), entry $3,000, stop $2,850 ($150 away):
→ $100 ÷ $150 = **0.67 ETH** (about $2,000 position)

If that position feels too small, the fix is to find a better entry. Moving the stop closer just to get a bigger position is not the fix.

## 4. Things to avoid

- **Round numbers and obvious lows** like $3,000 or $2,900. Liquidity collects there and price often sweeps those levels. Leave some room past them.
- **Leverage:** Make sure your liquidation price is well beyond your stop. If you'd be liquidated before the stop triggers, you're using too much leverage.
- **Moving the stop lower** after you're in the trade. You can tighten it as price moves your way, but don't widen it.
- **Check reward vs. risk.** Your first target should be at least 2× the stop distance away. If it isn't, the setup may not be worth taking.
