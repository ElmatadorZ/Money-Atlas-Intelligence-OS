**Short answer:** It's cheaper than it was, and cheap compared with its own history, but only if the "E" in that P/E holds up. A P/E below its average doesn't prove anything is cheap. It's a question about earnings.

📍 **MARKET STRUCTURE INSIGHT**

The arithmetic, using your numbers:
- 13 vs 16 means the index is priced about **19% below** its 10-year average multiple. Your earnings yield is 1/13 ≈ **7.7%**, against 1/16 ≈ 6.25% on average.
- If earnings stay flat and the multiple goes back to 16, that's about **+23%** (16/13).
- The other way to get back to 16 is for earnings to **fall about 19%** with the price unchanged. Then the index was never cheap, and the low P/E was just early warning of an earnings cut.
- If earnings were flat through the drop, the P/E was about 15.3 three months ago (13/0.85). So most of the "discount" comes from the recent sell-off, not from a long period of low valuation.

Two things I can't tell from here, and both decide the answer:
1. **Trailing or forward P/E?** A trailing P/E uses last year's earnings. If analysts are cutting forecasts, the forward P/E could already be close to 16.
2. **Is the 10-year average still a fair benchmark?** The SET leans heavily on energy, banks and old-economy sectors, growth is slow, and foreign investors have been net sellers for long stretches (as far as my data goes, to mid-2026). If that has permanently lowered the multiple the market deserves, 13 could be the new normal and not a discount.

I also don't have today's Thai bond yields. A 7.7% earnings yield looks a lot better against a 2% bond than a 4% one, so check the current 10-year Thai government bond yield.

📍 **KEY RISK**

A value trap: earnings fall and the index's P/E goes back to 16 at the same price or lower. **Invalidation:** the cheap case is dead if the next one or two quarters bring earnings downgrades of roughly 15–20%, or if the forward P/E turns out to be near 16 already.

📍 **STRATEGIC TAKEAWAY**

- **Real discount:** earnings estimates hold or rise, foreign selling slows, and the P/E moves back up from 13. Trigger: the next earnings season comes in without broad downgrades.
- **Value trap:** the price fell first and earnings cuts are following. Trigger: falling forward EPS estimates and margin pressure in the big sectors (energy, banks).

If you invest, the low multiple argues for buying gradually (staged buying) rather than all at once. Watch the next earnings revisions before committing more. How much to put in depends on your time horizon and how much of your money is already in Thai stocks.
