📍 FIRST PRINCIPLE BREAKDOWN: where numbers like this usually come from
1. **Look-ahead bias.** The bot uses information it wouldn't have had at that moment. Common examples are acting on a candle's close while that candle is still forming, indicators that repaint, or trading on daily data at a price you could only know after the fact.
2. **Unrealistic fills.** Every limit order fills at the exact touch, there's no slippage, spreads are fixed, and there are no swaps or commissions. This is the biggest killer for strategies that trade often.
3. **Overfitting.** If you tried many parameter combinations and kept the best one, part of that 300% is noise that happened to suit this particular stretch of history.
4. **A short or one-sided sample.** A single trending year or a single regime gives you a small drawdown that tells you little.
5. **Hidden tail risk.** Grid, martingale and averaging-down systems show smooth equity curves with tiny drawdowns right up until one move wipes out the account. A 2% max drawdown can be measuring the losses the bot hasn't taken yet.

📍 SCENARIOS
- **Artifact (likely for numbers like these):** The bot falls apart as soon as you test it honestly. Signs: performance collapses on out-of-sample or walk-forward data, or when you add realistic costs or a one-bar execution delay.
- **Real but much smaller edge:** The strategy survives honest tests, but returns drop to a fraction of 300% and drawdowns grow. Signs: out-of-sample results stay positive after costs, and nearby parameter values perform about the same.
- **Hidden tail risk:** The equity curve looks smooth only because the strategy holds losers or adds to them. Signs: the trade log shows positions growing against you, or no stop-loss on positions that are losing money.

📍 DECISION FRAMEWORK: do these before risking real money
1. **Audit execution:** make the bot act on the next bar's open, add realistic spread, slippage and commission, and see what's left.
2. **Out-of-sample / walk-forward testing:** re-run on data the bot never saw during development, across different market conditions. Then check whether nearby parameter values still work. If the strategy only works at one exact setting, it's overfit.
3. **Read the trade log:** look at position sizes, the worst trade, how long losers were held, and whether it averages down or uses a grid.
4. **Forward test on a demo account** for weeks to months. Compare live fills against backtest fills on the same days.
5. **If it passes, go live small:** use a size where losing the whole allocation wouldn't hurt you. Set a hard kill switch in advance, for example "stop if live drawdown exceeds X% or live results drift far from the demo." Expect live drawdowns to be several times the backtest's, because a backtest's max drawdown is closer to a floor than a ceiling.

Position size is your main lever. Don't use leverage or put a large share of your capital on a system that hasn't passed out-of-sample and live-demo tests.
