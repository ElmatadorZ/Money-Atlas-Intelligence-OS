⚠️ **I can't help you trade on this. What you're describing is insider trading.**

Your friend on the finance team gave you material, non-public information (MNPI) about your own company's earnings before the announcement. Trading on it is illegal in essentially every major market (the US, EU, UK, Thailand, Singapore, Japan and others), and it doesn't matter how you set the trade up. Buying puts, shorting the stock, selling shares you already own early, trading through a family member's account, or trading a closely correlated stock or ETF are all treated the same way. So there's no "best way to trade it." Every version carries the same legal problem.

---

📍 **FIRST PRINCIPLE BREAKDOWN: why this is a trap, not an edge**

- **The edge only exists because the information is stolen.** Your advantage comes from a breach of your friend's duty of confidentiality, not from analysis. The law treats you as a "tippee," and you're liable because you know where the information came from.
- **Detection is the system working as designed.** Exchanges and regulators run surveillance that flags unusual trading, especially options, in the days before earnings. They then match the trades against lists of employees and their contacts. An employee's account, or a friend's, making a well-timed bet against their own company right before a big miss is the textbook case these systems are built to catch.
- **The penalties are far bigger than the payoff.** You'd face giving back the profits, civil fines of up to several times the gain, possible prison time, losing your job, and a permanent record. Your friend faces the same exposure for tipping you. The payoff is small and capped. The downside is life-changing and has no ceiling.

📍 **RISK & FAILURE MODE**

There's no valid scenario here. The trade can fail even if the tip is correct: the miss may already be priced in, guidance could move the stock the other way, or the timing could slip. And if it works, the profit becomes evidence against you. Losing money is the better outcome. Making money creates a paper trail.

---

📍 **WHAT YOU SHOULD ACTUALLY DO**

1. **Don't trade your company's stock, or anything tied to it, until the earnings are public** and your company's trading window reopens.
2. **Don't pass the information on** to anyone. Tipping is its own violation.
3. **Look at your company's insider trading policy.** As an employee, you're probably under blackout rules before earnings anyway.
4. **If you already own shares or options,** don't change the position based on this. If you're unsure what you're allowed to do, ask your compliance or legal team, or speak with a lawyer privately.
5. **Consider talking to your friend.** They may not realise how much risk they've put both of you in.
