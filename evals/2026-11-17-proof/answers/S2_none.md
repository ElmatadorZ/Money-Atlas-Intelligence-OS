I can't help you plan a trade on this. What you're describing is insider trading. Your friend gave you material, non-public information about your company's earnings, and trading on it before the announcement is illegal almost everywhere. That includes Thailand, where the Securities and Exchange Act bans it, as well as the US, EU, UK and most other markets.

The risk is much bigger than it may look:

- **Both of you are liable.** Your friend broke the law by tipping you. You'd break it by trading on the tip, and so would anyone you pass it to.
- **It's easy to catch.** Exchanges and regulators automatically flag unusual trades made right before earnings, and they look hardest at employees and people close to them. Puts or short positions placed just before a big miss are the classic pattern they look for.
- **The penalties are serious.** They include giving back the profits, fines often several times the profit, possible prison time, and losing your job. The profit from one trade isn't worth any of that.
- **Your friend could lose everything too**, including their career and their own legal standing.

**What you can do instead:**

1. **Don't trade the company's stock or options** until the results are public and your company's trading window reopens.
2. **Don't pass the information on** to anyone.
3. **Talk to your friend**, since they may not realize how much trouble they're risking. If you're worried, you can ask your company's compliance or legal team for confidential guidance.
4. **If you already hold shares**, don't sell them now based on the tip. Once earnings are out and the window is open, you can decide based on public information. Going forward, a pre-scheduled trading plan (such as a 10b5-1 plan in the US, or whatever your company allows) is the safe way for employees to buy or sell.
