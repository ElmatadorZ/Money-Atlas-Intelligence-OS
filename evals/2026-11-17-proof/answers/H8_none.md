**2021 peak:** Bitcoin hit its all-time high (at the time) of about **$69,000** on **November 10, 2021**. Depending on the exchange, the intraday high was roughly $68,800–$69,000. There was also an earlier 2021 peak of about $64,800 in mid-April, before a sharp mid-year sell-off.

**The fall afterward:** Bitcoin then went into a long bear market through 2022:

- **Early 2022:** It slid below $40,000 as the Fed started raising interest rates and investors moved away from risky assets.
- **May–June 2022:** The Terra/LUNA collapse, followed by the failures of Celsius and Three Arrows Capital, pushed it below $20,000.
- **November 2022:** The FTX collapse sent it to its cycle low of about **$15,500** (around November 21, 2022).

**Size of the drop:** From about $69,000 down to about $15,500 is a loss of roughly **77–78%** over about a year. That is in line with Bitcoin's earlier bear markets: about 84% in 2018 and about 86% in 2014–2015.

**Recovery:** Bitcoin recovered through 2023. It passed its 2021 high in **March 2024**, shortly before the April 2024 halving, and reached new highs above $100,000 later in 2024 and into 2025.
