Not based on those numbers alone. A 25% vs 7% gap looks decisive, but a 3-year track record is weak evidence. Before you move money, check these:

**1. Is the comparison fair?**
- **Same asset type?** If the 25% fund holds stocks (or a single sector, region, or crypto) and your bank fund is a bond or mixed fund, you're comparing different risk levels, not different skill. Compare each fund to its own benchmark index over the same period.
- **Same 3 years?** If the market as a whole was up 20%+ a year, a 25% fund mostly rode the market.
- **Is the 25% after the fee?** Check whether it's net of the 2% fee and whether the bank fund's 7% is net of its own fees.

**2. Three years is too short.**
Funds at the top over a short period often don't stay there. Strong recent performance usually reflects a hot sector or a concentrated bet that can reverse. Look for:
- 5- to 10-year returns, ideally including a bad year like 2022
- The worst drawdown (largest peak-to-trough fall). A fund that returns 25% can also fall 40%.
- Whether the same manager and strategy produced the whole track record

**3. The 2% fee matters more than it looks.**
2% a year is high. Low-cost index funds often charge 0.1–0.5%. Over 20 years, a 2% fee can take roughly a third of your ending balance compared with a near-zero fee. Also check front-end or back-end loads and any exit fee on your current fund.

**4. Count what switching costs you.**
Redemption fees, taxes on gains when you sell, and time out of the market all count.

**5. Does it fit you?**
How long until you need the money? Could you hold on, without selling, through a 30–40% drop? If not, a high-volatility fund is the wrong choice whatever its past returns.

**A sensible middle path**
- Don't move everything at once. If you still like the fund after checking the points above, move a portion and keep the rest diversified.
- Also look at a low-cost, broad index fund. For most people it beats both a 2%-fee active fund and a typical bank fund over the long run, mainly because it costs less.

**Bottom line:** A 25% return over 3 years tells you what happened, not what will happen. Decide based on risk, fees, the long-term record against its benchmark, and your own time horizon. If you share both fund names or factsheets, I can compare them point by point.
