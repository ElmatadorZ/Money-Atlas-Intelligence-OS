**Short answer: it's cheaper than it was, and cheaper than its own history. That doesn't yet mean it's cheap.** A P/E of 13 against a 10-year average of 16 is a 19% discount. Whether that's a bargain or a fair price depends on three things the headline number doesn't show.

**1. Which "E" are you looking at?**
- If 13 is a **trailing** P/E, it uses last year's earnings. If profits are about to fall (weak exports, tourism, consumption, or margin pressure on banks and energy), the forward P/E could be 14–15 or higher. Then the discount mostly disappears.
- A quick check: if earnings hadn't changed, a 15% price drop would take a P/E of about 15.3 down to 13. So the derating is almost all price, and the market may simply be pricing in earnings cuts that haven't been reported yet. **Check whether analysts' forward EPS estimates are being revised down.** If they are, the stock may be a value trap, not a value.

**2. Is the 10-year average still the right benchmark?**
Averages only matter if the conditions behind them still hold. For the SET, several things have changed:
- **Slower structural growth**: Thai GDP growth trend is low, the population is aging, and the investment cycle is weak.
- **Index mix**: the index leans heavily on energy, banks, and old-economy sectors, with few high-growth companies. Markets like that tend to trade at lower multiples permanently.
- **Foreign outflows and governance concerns** have weighed on valuations for years.
If the "new normal" P/E is about 13–14, then 13 is fair value, not a discount.

**3. Compare it with interest rates, not just with its own past.**
A P/E of 13 means an earnings yield of about 7.7%. Compare that with the yield on Thai government bonds. The gap between the two (the equity risk premium) shows whether you're actually being paid for the risk. A wide gap supports "cheap." A narrow one means low P/E alone isn't enough.

**Other things to check**
- P/B and dividend yield compared with their history (dividend yield in particular is a strong anchor for the SET)
- Foreign fund flows: is selling still going on, or has it started to dry up?
- Breadth: is the whole market cheap, or are a few big sectors pulling the average down?

**Bottom line**
- **Cheap vs. history**: yes.
- **Cheap vs. future earnings and interest rates**: not proven yet.
- **How to act on it**: don't try to catch the bottom all at once. If you believe the long-term story, build a position in stages (DCA or tranches) and add more when earnings revisions stop falling. A low P/E tells you the odds are getting better. It doesn't tell you when the price will turn.
