**Where to get the exact number:**
- **U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (bls.gov)**: the "Consumer Price Index News Release" for September 2026. The September report usually comes out in mid-October. Look for "All items, 12-month percent change, not seasonally adjusted". That is the headline year-over-year figure.
- **FRED (St. Louis Fed)**: series CPIAUCNSA. Take September 2026 divided by September 2025, minus 1. That gives you the same year-over-year number and lets you check it.

**Check you're comparing the same thing:**
- **Headline vs core:** headline CPI includes food and energy. Core CPI leaves them out. News stories often quote both, so check which one a source means.
- **CPI vs PCE:** the Fed's 2% target is set on PCE inflation, not CPI. The two measures usually differ by a few tenths of a point.
- **Revisions:** the non-seasonally-adjusted year-over-year figure is generally not revised after release. The seasonally adjusted monthly figures are revised each February. If two sources disagree, one of them may be quoting a different series.
